Synthetic Biology and BioProcess Engineering Laboratory

We build yeast, microalgae, and bacteria into production platforms for sustainable biomanufacturing — combining pathway design, genome engineering, and fermentation to convert CO2, methane, methanol, and lignocellulosic sugars into value-added products.

Research

Five programs, one through-line: take a carbon source nobody wants, and engineer a cell that turns it into something worth selling.

Foundational platform

Synthetic biology of diverse microorganisms

Synthetic biology is a multidisciplinary field for creating microorganisms with a purpose. We develop the genetic engineering techniques that make non-conventional hosts tractable — CRISPR/Cas9 editing, Golden Gate cloning, safe-harbor knock-in, and in vivo DNA assembly — and pair them with high-throughput screening so that engineered strains can be obtained efficiently rather than one at a time.

design → build → test → learn
integration efficiency 15.3% → 68.1% nine-fragment assembly 86.5% FACS-based hotspot screening

Nannochloropsis · Chlamydomonas · Chlorella

Value-added products from CO2 using photosynthetic microorganisms

Microalgae fix CO2 directly. We manipulate the carbon concentrating mechanism and CO2 fixation pathways to push carbon toward lipids, omega-3 fatty acids, and carotenoids, then combine batch, semi-batch, and continuous cultivation with controlled stress to hold productivity and quality together.

CO₂ → Calvin cycle → TAG, EPA, carotenoids
lipid productivity +71% EPA 14.4 mg L−1 d−1

Saccharomyces · Issatchenkia

Value-added products from lignocellulosic biomass using yeast

Lignocellulosic hydrolysate is roughly 70% glucose and 30% xylose, and yeast will not eat both at once. We engineer sugar transport and central metabolism to break glucose repression, exploit acid tolerance to ferment without pH adjustment, and use genome-scale modeling and metabolic reconstruction to keep the process economically viable.

glucose + xylose → malate, lactate, fatty acids
malic acid 61.2 g L−1 lactic acid 67 g L−1, self-buffering

Algal–bacterial consortia

Integrated bioprocesses for diverse products from CO2

A single strain rarely does both jobs well. We design modular co-cultures that pair a photosynthetic partner with bacteria or yeast, splitting a pathway across organisms: a photorespiration mutant of C. reinhardtii secretes glycolic acid from CO2, and the heterotrophic module converts it into the actual product in a two-stage continuous system.

CO₂ → glycolate → lycopene, protein
lycopene 6.3 µg L−1 h−1 two-stage continuous culture

Methanotrophs · Komagataella phaffii

Methanotrophs and methylotrophic yeast for C1 chemicals

Methane and methanol are abundant one-carbon feedstocks, but their hosts pay for them in toxicity, redox imbalance, and carbon lost to dissimilation. We engineer C1 assimilation pathways in methanotrophs and methylotrophic yeast, mitigate formaldehyde and oxidative stress, and redirect the pyruvate node toward secreted products.

CH₄, methanol → formaldehyde → lactate, terpenoids
63.8 mg lactate / g methanol synthetic methylotrophy
